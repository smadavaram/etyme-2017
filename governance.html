Governance

Where a limit is legally grounded, Etyme blocks and says why. Everywhere else it warns, takes a reason and lets you proceed. Nothing is silently allowed, and every override keeps the name of whoever gave it.

Key capabilities

Blocks where the law is behind it

Tenure cap, break in service, work authorization, lapsed supplier insurance, a person signing their own.

Warns everywhere else

Rate band, headcount plan, supplier tier. A reason is recorded and the work proceeds.

Every decision written down

Rule, subject, outcome and reason, even a pass. An override keeps the name of whoever gave it.

L0 to L5

Every unprompted action graded, from “looked and told somebody” to “acted because a rule said so”.

Reversible by a person

What the system did on its own is listed, with a reverse button where it can be put back.

Reads are logged

Every read of another person’s data writes a row, refusals included.

The complaint

“Our last system had forty rules and every one of them was a pop-up somebody clicked past. When the auditor asked who approved the exception, the honest answer was: the system let it through.”

The compliance desk at a company that has already bought one system

A rule that can be clicked past is a suggestion with a log entry, and the log rarely says who. Systems get here by treating every check the same: a warning for the tenure limit, a warning for the rate band, a warning for the lapsed insurance, until nobody reads any of them.

What Etyme does

Every check is sorted into one of two kinds before it is written. Where the law is behind a limit, Etyme blocks: the tenure cap, a break in service, work authorization, a supplier’s lapsed insurance, a person approving their own work. The block is a sentence that says why, and nobody can wave it through.

Everywhere else it warns, records a reason and proceeds: a rate outside the band, a headcount plan, a supplier below the tier procurement set. Every decision is written down, even a pass, with the name of whoever overrode it. What the system does on its own is graded L0 to L5, from “it looked and told somebody” to “it acted because a written rule said so”, and approving a timesheet without a person is off until your company turns it on.

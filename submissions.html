Submissions & screening

Every supplier submits against the same role, and each person arrives on one screen with the firm that sent them and the rate it asked. Two firms sending the same person is caught before anyone is interviewed.

Key capabilities

One screen for every supplier

Each candidate arrives with the firm that sent them and the rate it asked.

Duplicates held

The same person twice on a requirement: first in wins, and the second firm is told.

Nine screening checks

Budget, work authorization, can start, supplier engaged, governance, not barred, skills evidenced, worked here before, already submitted.

Rate against the band

Outside the supplier’s band warns. Above the ceiling warns. Neither blocks a submission.

Interviews, three ways

Nothing is booked until the client, the supplier and the consultant have all said so.

Award takes the position

One person, one position, the order that pays for it. The last position filled stands the other suppliers down.

The complaint

“Four agencies sent us the same candidate at four different rates, and two of them are now arguing about whose he is. Meanwhile the one we actually wanted is at the bottom of somebody’s inbox.”

A hiring manager at a company with more than one supplier

Suppliers compete, and that is the point of having more than one. But when each submission is an email, the client does the reconciling: which person is which, who sent them first, and whether the rate on the résumé is the rate on the invoice.

What Etyme does

A supplier can submit only to a role it was sent, only while its insurance is in date, and only a person who agreed to be represented by it. The submission lands on one screen for the client, with the firm that sent it and the rate it asked. The same person submitted twice to a requirement is held: first in wins, and the second firm is told so.

Screening reads every arrival against the same nine checks: in budget, already submitted, work authorization, can start, supplier engaged, governance, not barred, skills evidenced, worked here before. Then shortlist, interview, award. Nothing is booked until the client, the supplier and the consultant have all said so. An award takes a position, and when the last position is taken the other suppliers are stood down.

What it refuses

R. Adebayo has already been submitted to this requirement First submission wins. Keystone Talent is told it was second, rather than finding out at the interview.
$124 an hour is outside Keystone Talent’s band for this role A warning, not a refusal. The supplier can submit, and the client sees the rate against the band before shortlisting.

Only the client can place a candidate; a supplier cannot award its own. A rejection needs a sentence saying why, because it is the only thing that makes the next submission better. And a firm cannot submit a person at all unless that person agreed to be represented by it, unless they are the firm’s own employee.

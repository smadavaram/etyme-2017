The chain

Your supplier, its subcontractor, the firm that actually employs the person: every hop is recorded, each firm sees only the rung it buys and sells at, and hours are filed once at the bottom and billed up the chain from the same signed week.

Key capabilities

Every hop recorded

Client, prime, subcontractor, employer. A chain of contracts, each firm buying from the one below.

One rate per rung

Each firm sees the rate it pays and the rate it charges, and no other. The client sees the rung it pays.

Names private, papers not

Sub-vendor names stay private unless the agreement discloses them. Insurance and work authorization never hide.

Hours filed once

On the bottom rung. Each firm above bills the same signed week at its own rate.

Forwarded without a price

A candidate sent up the chain carries no rate. A lower onward rate warns.

Prime here, sub there

The same firm can be either, on different sites, in one record.

The complaint

“We pay one agency. The person on site has an email address from a company we have never heard of. When she was hurt, three firms each said she was somebody else’s.”

The risk desk at a company that buys through a prime supplier

Subcontracting is normal and mostly fine. The prime keeps the relationship; a smaller firm has the person. What goes wrong is that the client knows one name, the invoice carries one rate, and nobody has written down who employs the person or who insured them.

What Etyme does

A placement is a chain of contracts. Each firm sells to the one above and buys from the one below, and the link is empty where a firm employs the person itself. The record is read downward and never upward, so each firm sees the rate it pays and the rate it charges and no other, and the client sees the rung it pays. Names below the prime stay private unless the agreement discloses them. Insurance and work authorization status are never hidden, at any depth.

Hours are filed once, by the person, on the bottom rung. Once the client approves the week, each firm above bills the same week at its own rate, and an invoice line exists once per week per contract. A candidate sent up the chain goes on without its price. The same firm can be a prime on one site and a sub on another, and the record does not mind.

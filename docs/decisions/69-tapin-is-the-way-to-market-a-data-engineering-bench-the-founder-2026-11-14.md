## Tapin is the way to market: a data engineering bench — the founder, 2026-11-14

Asked what the public pages should say about Etyme and a bench, now that
Tapin is his (record 68), the founder answered: *"Tapin is my way to
market - tapin is going to run bench primarily focussed on Data
engineering. Clients should not know we train and place it should be
something my bench recruiting division handles through backchannel and
referrals today but make it easy for them"*.

**What stands.**

1. **Tapin is how Etyme reaches the market.** It is a bench firm, mainly
   in data engineering. It runs on Etyme as a supplier like any other
   and gets no access another supplier lacks (record 28). That part does
   not move.
2. **Nothing a client reads says that Tapin trains people, or places
   them, or is the founder's.** No public page, no client's screen, no
   email to a client. This widens the older rule that a client's view
   never says "training" (record 56).
3. **No page claims the opposite either.** No sentence says Etyme runs
   no bench, places nobody, or has no interest in any supplier. With
   Tapin that would not be true, and every public sentence must be one a
   reader can check (record 03). The pages say what the product does and
   say nothing on this point.
4. **Candidates come in by backchannel and referral**, through Tapin's
   own recruiters: a message to somebody they know, a referral from a
   consultant on the bench, a referral from another recruiter. The
   product makes that easy. It is not advertised to clients.
5. **Every existing rule about a person holds**: she joins a bench only
   by her own yes; nobody gets a seat or a second record before it; a
   firm's page never shows a name or a rate; a bench is shared only
   between firms that already trade.

**The coordinator's caution, said to him once.** Saying nothing is his
choice to make. Two moments need a true answer ready, because the
product sells on trust: an enterprise buyer who asks directly whether
Etyme's owner has an interest in any supplier; and a client's program
that Etyme runs as a vendor-neutral MSP with Tapin among its suppliers.
In the second, the client is told, or Tapin stays out of that program.
He decides which.

**Not built yet.** A read of the code, started the same day, says how a
recruiter brings a candidate in by a pasted link today, how a consultant
or another recruiter refers one, where "Data engineering" would be said
on Tapin's own page, and every place a client could read that a supplier
trains or runs a bench. The build follows from that read and from the
walk of Tapin's first payroll cycle (record 68).

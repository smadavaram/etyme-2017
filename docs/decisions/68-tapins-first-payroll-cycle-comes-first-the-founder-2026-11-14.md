## Tapin's first payroll cycle comes first — the founder, 2026-11-14

After the first deploy of the wave went out (record 67), the founder
answered four questions. His words, as typed:

1. On whether Tapin had imported people with rates: *"No tapin has not
   imported, plan is to add invites to candidates and they will self
   onboard. Then imitate contracts that wil be reduced hr documentation
   with focus on running the payrolls in ne t cycle."* Read as: invite
   candidates; they onboard themselves; contracts are then started on
   reduced HR documentation; the aim is to run payroll in the next
   cycle.
2. On whether one payroll person may attach the client's approval and
   accept the same week: *"Yes in some cases but most of the time
   candidate will send to timesheets@etyme. Com and they be bcc to
   tapin.etyme.com account"*.
3. On the bench vendor's page and the line "Etyme runs no bench and
   places nobody": *"We changed rule when tapin discussion started. We
   wanted tapin both for fixing the app and next marketing or partnering
   with other recruiters to market."*
4. On starting the next deploy: *"You deploy when green. It's been long
   wait And worried what you built this week that took so long"*.

**What stands from this.**

- **The order of work is Tapin's own first cycle**: invite candidates,
  self-onboarding, a contract on the fewest papers, a week accepted,
  payroll in the next cycle. It is his rule already ("whatever stops a
  firm's real first week", record 56). The coding block (64) and the
  party pages (63) wait behind it.
- **Reduced HR documentation means the floor, and no more.** A missing
  I-9 or right to work, lapsed insurance and lapsed good standing block
  (record 49). Everything else warns, takes a reason and proceeds. Where
  the product asks for more than that before a start, it is a fault.
- **One payroll person may attach the client's approval and accept the
  same week.** No second person is required. The approval still names a
  person at the client (record 46).
- **No rows need correcting** from the import fault found on 2026-11-14:
  Tapin never imported people with rates.
- **"Deploy when green" stands** for the next deploy (record 65).

**Not decided, and nothing is built on it.**

- **Timesheets by email.** A candidate writing to the firm's timesheets
  mailbox with a copy to the firm's Etyme address is email reaching
  Etyme. That door exists and is closed by his own rule (record 31):
  Etyme never reads a mailbox; only a seated colleague's forwarded
  message is accepted; nothing becomes a record without a person's yes
  on a card; and it stays off until his attack walk and his yes. To do
  what he describes, two yeses are his: that a worker's own message
  about her own week is accepted, as a card for the payroll person; and
  that the door is switched on after the attack walk.
- **What the public pages say about Etyme and a bench.** The standing
  rule says Etyme never runs a bench and never places anybody, and that
  Tapin is a supplier like any other (records 03, 28). He says that
  changed when Tapin began, and that Tapin is for fixing the app and for
  marketing with other recruiters. So no public page claims "Etyme runs
  no bench and places nobody" until he says what it should say. Inside
  the product nothing moves: Tapin gets no access another supplier
  lacks.

**Decided the same day: a timesheet never comes by email.** Told that
his second answer meant email reaching Etyme, and that the door is
closed by his own rule, he answered: *"You are right timesheets by email
is unnecessary - they should come through Etyme or ask Etyme
harnessed"*. So the email door stays closed and nothing about it is
asked again. A candidate files her week, with the timesheet her client
approved, on her own page or in Ask Etyme. Where the candidate does send
her firm the approved timesheet some other way, the firm's payroll
person attaches it for her, as he may. Of the first bullet above, only
this remains to build: filing the week and attaching the client's
approval as one step, from her page and from Ask Etyme.

**The week, said honestly to him.** Shipped: the twenty-two first-day
faults, the Owner rules, the neutral Company, sign-in and sign-up to his
drawing, the home page on realistic numbers, a client's codes kept the
client's, and the contractor's week with the client's approval attached.
Why it took a week: three stops on the usage allowance, one of eleven
hours; a rule file every agent had to read that had grown to 84,000
words; three rounds of walking on screens, each of which found real
faults in work called built; and the coordinator running more at once
than the task needed. Little of the week was on Tapin's payroll path,
which is why that path is walked next.

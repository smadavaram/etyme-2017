## Sensitive numbers are masked until a reveal is granted — 2026-11-18

**What he said** (message 132, as formalized for him and sent on). It
covers a tax ID, bank details and a social security number.
1. **Masked and encrypted, always.** Stored encrypted and shown masked
   on every screen, for everyone, the Owner included, until a reveal is
   granted.
2. **The Owner's reveal.** Click to display, but only after signing in
   again at that moment: the same second check as a money act, passkey
   first and the authenticator app as fallback.
3. **Anyone else.** She asks, the Owner approves, and the value shows
   only inside the window the Owner grants. Then it is masked again.
   Nothing is unmasked for good.
4. **Sharing outside** (a payroll provider, an e-signature app). The
   owner of record confirms digitally. The file or form that carries
   the data is attached to the record with who confirmed, what was
   shared, with whom and when.
5. **Every reveal and every share is on the ledger**, on the record.

It stacks on the model rule: this data reaches no model on any
provider (records 81, 96).

**How it meets the standing rules.**
- It sharpens record 87, which said "in full only to a permitted seat
  after a fresh second check, each showing logged". The permitted seat
  is now the Owner, or a seat the Owner grants a window to.
- An Admin still neither enters nor confirms bank details (record 86),
  and is not the one who grants a reveal.
- The second-check core and the append-only security log exist (records
  88, 90). The sealed papers and the reveal are not built.

**Asked of him before it is built.**
- "The owner of record" in rule 4: the company's Owner, or the person
  whose number it is? Recommended: both. The person consents to her own
  number leaving, and the Owner confirms the firm sends it.
- A worker reading her own number: the same second check, with no
  approval from anybody. Recommended: yes.
- The length of a granted window. Recommended: fifteen minutes, one
  record, one seat.
- A company with several Owners: any one may grant, and never to
  herself without the second check.

### Who asks, and why — 2026-11-18

He confirmed rule 3: the recruiter asks, the Owner approves, and the
recruiter then receives it. And he widened who asks: "not only the
recruiter but HR admin, or the account manager at the client, or the
background check company, or E-Verify or I-9 verification, or
contracts, the accounting system, or a federal requirement."

- The reason is a purpose picked from a list, never free text alone:
  I-9 and work authorization, the federal employment check, a
  background check, payroll setup, a tax form, the accounting system, a
  contract or a client's site requirement, a government requirement,
  other (with words).
- Two kinds of asker. A seat inside the firm reads the value inside a
  window (rule 3). A party outside the firm (the client's account
  manager, a background check provider, a government service, the
  accounting system) never reads it in Etyme: it is a share (rule 4),
  confirmed and attached to the record.
- Recommended, for his yes: the Owner may grant a standing purpose to a
  role, so HR does I-9 and payroll setup without a request each time.
  Each reveal still asks HR's own second check and is on the ledger. A
  rule slower than the workaround produces the workaround.

He agreed that a client reading a worker's number is the riskiest case,
and added two more purposes: "maybe a lawyer needs it, or the employee
is moving to the client's employment."
- A lawyer: a share to named counsel, for a stated matter, confirmed by
  the Owner. The worker is told, unless counsel says the law forbids
  telling her.
- A worker moving to the client's employment: her numbers are hers. She
  sends her own papers to her new employer from her own page, by her
  own yes. The firm does not send them for her.

## The sign-in page leads with the account buttons, in our own branding — the founder, 2026-11-12, evening

On photographs of the email-first page built that morning: *"No, it's
not looking good at all."* He showed the sign-in page of another
product and said its flow was better, then said exactly what he meant:
*"Stick to the branding we had. I didn't mean colors. I meant how a new
user signs up, or Google sign in, or Microsoft sign in, or LinkedIn sign
in, and then if the user wants to use username and password or
directory sign in."* And on the two filled buttons: *"The blue became
very very prominent. When I said prominent I didn't mean that."* Drawn
in Etyme's branding (https://claude.ai/artifact/1kL7UnwkJR13wDqZFZMUGh,
version 2). This replaces "The sign-in page asks for the email first"
and "Sign up is as prominent as sign in" of the same day.
- **The flow, in order.** The account buttons first: Continue with
  Google, Microsoft, LinkedIn, each drawn only where its key exists, the
  door this browser used last first. Then "or use your email": one
  Email box and Continue, for a password or a company's own directory.
  The password box appears only after the email, and only for an
  account that has one.
- **Sign up is one line under the heading**, the live page's own "New
  here? Sign up", and the same screen: the account buttons, or an email. By
  email it asks a name, company or candidate, the company's name, what
  it does and a password, and nothing else. An account button asks
  nothing: the first screen inside asks what it needs.
- **The branding does not move.** On a photograph of the page live on
  production: *"This page was good, just the flow was bad."* The
  wordmark, the serif heading, the warm ground, the existing fields and
  buttons. No dark ground, no new
  color. **One filled button on a screen**; every other button is an
  outline.
- **No button is greyed out for an empty field.** It can be pressed and
  says what is missing. Found on our own page, on the photographs.
- **What stays underneath:** a company on directory-only sign-in is
  sent to its directory whichever button its people press, and never
  sees a password box; the page never says whether an address has an
  account; the email is routing, never proof.
- **LinkedIn is for candidates only, and the button says so** (founder,
  the same evening: *"For LinkedIn, mention for candidate only"*). The
  button reads "Continue with LinkedIn" with "Candidates only" beside
  it. Somebody with a seat at a company is refused there in one sentence
  that says which button to use. Coordinator's reading, reversible in a
  sentence: this narrows the rule of 2026-11-11, which also let a
  one-person firm use LinkedIn; a one-person firm now signs in with
  Google, Microsoft or a password.
- **The lesson, the fourth time on this page:** a sign-in page is
  photographed at phone width and shown to the founder before it is
  called built. Words in a reply did not catch a greyed button, two
  filled buttons and a six-field sign-up; five photographs did.
- Built by the architect to the drawing, after the first-day fixes
  report back; photographs to the founder before any deploy.

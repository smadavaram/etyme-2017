## A role is requested, assigned and approved by three people — 2026-11-18

**What he said.** Asked whether the person who assigns a role should
differ from the person who approves it: "A manager or employee requests
a role for themselves or their team member, IT assigns, their manager
approves, live."

**The flow.**
1. **Request.** A person asks for a role for herself, or a manager asks
   for a member of her team. The request names the role and, for cover,
   the dates (record 99).
2. **Assign.** The Admin, who is IT and people, sets it up: the person,
   the role, the dates. Nothing is live yet.
3. **Approve.** The manager of the person who will hold the role says
   yes. Then it is live.

Three steps, and no person takes two of them for the same request.
Every step is on the ledger with its name.

**Where it meets the standing rules, for his yes.**
- "A box's lead grants nothing" (team structure). This flow lets a
  manager approve a role, which that rule forbade. The rule is read now
  as: a lead grants nothing by herself, and approves a role an Admin
  has assigned.
- "The Owner decides who sees what." Recommended: the manager approves
  an ordinary role. The Owner approves any role that carries money,
  sensitive numbers (record 99), or the right to grant access.
- "A role nobody has named falls back; it never refuses." Where the
  person has no manager on the team structure, the Owner approves.
- Nobody approves her own request. A manager asking for herself goes to
  her own manager, or to the Owner.
- A one-person firm: the Owner does all three, logged, with the second
  check where the role carries money or sensitive numbers.
- A company on directory-only sign-in maps groups to roles in its own
  directory. Its approval happens there, and Etyme reads the result.

**Not built.** Today an Admin or the Owner gives a role directly in
Settings.

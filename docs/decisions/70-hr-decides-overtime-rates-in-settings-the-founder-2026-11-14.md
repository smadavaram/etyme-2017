## HR decides overtime rates in settings — the founder, 2026-11-14

Told that a walk of his firm's first pay cycle found a 46-hour week paid
at the usual rate by silence, and that a week over forty could not be
approved at a client that is not on Etyme, the founder answered: *"Give
the hr admin the settings to decide overtime rates - some clients pay
1.5 times but most pay same. The govt compliance is different in
different countries"*.

**What stands.**

1. **Overtime is a setting, and HR sets it.** The firm's HR seat (and
   the Owner or an Admin) decides the overtime rules under Settings.
   Nobody is asked to decide the same thing week after week. A setting
   holds a multiple, never a person's rate, so it does not break the
   rule that HR reads no money.
2. **What a client is billed for hours over the weekly line is set per
   client.** Most clients pay the same rate for those hours; some pay
   one and a half times. So the default is the same rate, and HR changes
   it for a client that pays more. A week still may depart from the
   client's setting, with a reason recorded.
3. **What a worker is paid for hours over the line follows the country
   where the work is done.** The law differs by country, so the rule is
   kept per country, and HR sets it. Where regulatory has confirmed a
   country's rule with its source, that rule is the starting value (for
   the United States: time and a half after forty hours in the week for
   a worker who is not exempt, record 46). Where it has not, the product
   offers no starting value, warns, and never blocks (record 49).
4. **Pay and billing are two settings.** What the client pays for the
   extra hours never decides what the worker is owed, and the reverse.
5. **Nothing is paid short by silence.** Until a person's overtime
   status is said, a week of hers over the line is held off the pay
   file with a sentence saying why. It is never worked out at the usual
   rate and left for somebody to notice.

**What this replaces.** Until now, whoever approved a week decided, week
by week, whether the hours over forty were billed at the usual rate or
at a premium. That question now starts from the client's setting. For a
client that is not on Etyme, the person attaching the client's approval
no longer has to choose each time: the setting answers, and the form
shows it.

**Not built yet.** It follows the fixes already in hand for the first
pay cycle: a way on screen to say whether a person is exempt, and a
flagged week at a client not on Etyme approved by the client's attached
approval. The order is: what exists today, read with its files; the
schema lines, through the architect; the rule written as test names;
the build; and every figure worked by hand by a reader who did not build
it, before a release check.

**Open, for regulatory, not for the founder.** Each country's rule with
its source, before the product offers it as a starting value.

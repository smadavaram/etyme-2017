## Tapin pays monthly, after the client's approved timesheet — 2026-11-18

**What he said.**
- "For November we don't receive timesheets until Dec 5 to 7, and then
  process at the end of the month."
- "Client approval is a timesheet. The payment terms for the client to
  clear would be 30, 45, 60 or 90 and sometimes 120. Tapin goes with
  timesheet approval and takes the risk and closes payments for November
  usually by Jan 1 to 5."
- "Clients pay much later, after 69 days."
- "Tapin is too small, but usually bigger companies above 10 people do
  biweekly."

**What it means.**
- The client's approval is the client's approved timesheet. Pay follows
  that approval, never the client's payment: Tapin carries the risk of a
  client that pays late.
- A client's terms are a number of days on its agreement: 30, 45, 60,
  90 or 120.
- A small firm pays once a month, in arrears: the month's work is paid
  early in the month after next (November by Jan 1 to 5). A larger firm
  pays every other week. Both are the company's choice, as the standing
  rule says. Every other week stays the default.

**What the advisers said, the same day.**
- Regulatory: for a W-2 employee, wages are due on the regular pay day,
  and a lag of about five weeks after the month ends is longer than any
  state limit it knows. For a contractor or a supplier firm it is an
  ordinary contract term. Each state's limit needs its source before
  anything is enforced, so today Etyme warns and never blocks.
- Money: "Once a month" exists, but its pay day sits inside the month
  and ends the period, so "November, paid Jan 5" cannot be set. Pay is
  gated on the client's approval first, which matches Tapin's practice.

**What is to be built** (not built yet).
- A pay period "once a month, paid N days after the month ends". The
  period is the calendar month. It needs one stored number, through the
  schema queue.
- Defaults, to be confirmed by him before they are relied on: a day
  belongs to the month it was worked in; overtime is worked out by the
  week and paid with the month the week ends in; the tax year follows
  the day paid; the weekend rule moves the pay day as it does today.
- A W-2 schedule that pays later than a confirmed state limit shows one
  warning sentence, with the reason captured. It never blocks the run.
- The settings panel says which days a pay day covers, and warns when
  the period is switched after payroll has run. Pay days already written
  never move.
- Tapin's first Sunday (Dec 27) stays stored and is unused while the
  period is monthly.
- Smaller, no decision needed: a reminder to a client with a week
  waiting, and a list of weeks waiting on the client, oldest first.

**Open.** Whether the people Tapin pays this way are W-2 employees or
contractors. Record 71 says its first people are W-2, paid every two
weeks. He was asked and has not said.

## The home page shows one sample company at a real buyer's scale — the founder, 2026-11-13

**The founder's words.** He uploaded a reference page of his own and
asked: *"Is this covered"*. It was not: the page had been built from the
brief's words and not to the reference's structure. Then, the same day:
*"How do we differentiate vendor invoices and customer invoices both in
language and business? Also we are talking about companies spending
40-50 million a year; make the numbers realistic"*. And on the first
held figure the rebuilt page showed: *"42 looks like money nobody cares
about"*.

**What stands.**

1. **The home page follows his reference page's structure**, in Etyme's
   own brand kit: header, hero with the "At a glance" panel, the Ask
   Etyme demo, the CFO view closed behind a button, "Held until it
   agrees" with three proof cards, the strip, price and security, and
   the closing band. Record 59 holds the brief.
2. **Every figure on the page is one sample company, Corvane Packaging,
   at the scale of a real buyer**, labeled "Demo — not a customer", and
   read from the seed through a function. Nothing is typed in. Northbend
   stays the story on every other public page.
3. **The scale.** 359 contractors on site through 25 suppliers at 31
   sites in 8 countries. Specialists, at one bill rate a country in its
   own currency: US $120, Belgium €95, Northern Ireland £72, Mexico MXN
   900, Brazil BRL 260, Colombia COP 180,000, Vietnam VND 950,000, India
   INR 3,200. At 160 hours a month and the sample exchange rates that is
   $3,615,443.20 a month and about $43.4 million a year.
4. **The time limit.** Aoife Kelly in Belfast has 17 whole months of 18,
   through two suppliers one after the other, and reaches the limit in
   14 days. The glance reads "17 / 18".
5. **The money held.** Seven invoice receipts are held this month, one
   or two in every region. Each is one supplier's weekly invoice for
   several of its contractors, billing hours nobody signed; no week is
   more than 12 hours over. Together they bill $17,344.56 over the
   signed hours, about half a percent of the month. The largest is at
   Dayton, $3,660 over. Every one is held by the three-way check on its
   hours and on nothing else.
6. **What is held is the invoice; the dollar figure is what it bills
   over the signed hours.** A reader checking the numbers found the page
   calling $17,344.56 "held". Each invoice is held whole, and the seven
   bill $97,889.36 in all. The page now says "7 supplier invoices held
   this month — $17,344.56 billed over the signed hours". A test holds
   the two apart.
7. **The words for an invoice.** Inside the product nothing moves: what
   a firm issues upward is a bill, and what it receives from below is an
   invoice receipt (record 48). The home page is written to a client,
   and to a client every invoice is a supplier's. His reference page
   says "supplier invoice", so the home page says it in full. Every
   other public page keeps "invoice receipt", and a test allows the
   phrase on the home page alone.

**Built.** 829644766 (the reference's structure), 4b02f96e9 (the

**Open, for the founder.**
- The rates outside the US and Europe are high for these jobs, and a
  finance reader may say so. Lowering them means more people or hours
  elsewhere to keep the year between 40 and 50 million dollars.
- The month's spend is an estimate at 160 hours and is shown to the
  cent.
- The page could lead with the invoices' whole value ($97,889.36 held)
  and say the overage second.

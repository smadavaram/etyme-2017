## The profit logic the pack must serve — 2026-11-21

**What it is.** A note from the tester, at the owner's direction, that
sits on top of the execution pack (record 105). The pack is the build
order; this is why. Where the two pull apart, the pack's guardrails
win: no agent sends, no agent decides money, and no agent acts across
companies. The named note stays outside the repository, as the pack
does.

**The arithmetic, with its assumptions named.** A placed consultant at
a $15 an hour margin over about 1,880 billable hours is about $28,000
to $30,000 of gross profit a year. One million dollars is then 33 to 36
consultant-years. At a $10 margin it is about 50. Holding the margin is
worth more than adding volume. These are the note's own model figures,
not a promise and not for any public page.

**Where the gap is.** Not supply and not demand: more requirements
arrive each day than the firm reads. The gap is conversion and leakage.
1. A rate confirmation is issued and no outcome is ever recorded.
2. A rate is won in writing and then drifts, by a vendor's notice.
3. Money is billed and does not arrive: old invoices, short pays,
   recoveries nobody decides.
4. The people who close spend their day on one-line clerical acts.

**The levers, in order, each already a pack item.**
1. Close the confirmation loop: every confirmation gets an outcome, so
   the firm knows its submission-to-placement rate.
2. Answer fast, on the record.
3. Hold the rate: agreed against actual, drift as a same-week decision.
4. Collect as one chain: no invoice past 60 days without a named owner
   and a next step.
5. Put the verified bench to work: each day's requirements matched
   against it, as a suggestion.
6. Agents do the clerical work; people close.

**The direction beyond this tranche** (not to be built now).
- The record is what lasts; a model can be rented by anybody. The pack
  is built so that history accumulates as a by-product of the work:
  every rate, outcome, drift and payment.
- Rate guidance at the moment of confirmation, from the firm's own
  outcomes. A person still confirms.
- Scorecards of who pays on time and who drifts, from the ledger.
- A chain that produces a measured result here becomes a standard
  offer.
- Never: an agent that sends or negotiates by itself; people's data
  scraped into a bench; a one-off workflow per client.

**To be measured from the ledger and reported with each batch:**
conversion (submissions to placements), rate drift caught, and the age
of money owed.

**Where it meets the standing rules.**
- "No feature ever compares a supplier's rates across clients." Rate
  guidance and scorecards are read only inside the one tenant, from its
  own records, and nothing crosses to another tenant. They are not in
  this tranche; before either is built, the founder says so.
- A result measured at one tenant is that tenant's. It is named on a
  public page only with its consent, and only as a figure a reader can
  check.

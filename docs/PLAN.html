The plan · one page

Sell finished work

You don't sell agents. You sell jobs that are done.

A company pays a fixed price per job. Your crew of agents and people does it. You keep the difference.

Read only this one. The two longer documents were the detailed version. This page replaces both. Same plan, fewer words.

1

The whole business, in one picture

A company sends you 1,000 jobs a month. Your crew does them. You send the finished work back. You invoice.

The crew is part agents, part people. The customer never sees the mix. That mix is your business.

2

You already built most of it

This is the important part. I looked through Etyme. It has 136 tables. Most of them are exactly what this business needs.

Timesheets with approval chains. Invoices. Client bills and vendor bills. Stripe. DocuSign. Contracts with rate cards. Vendor and sub-vendor chains. Background checks.

You built all of it for people. Agents need the same things. You need three new pieces. That's it.

Your head start is not AI. It is that you already know how work becomes an invoice becomes cash.

3

How you make money

Say the customer pays an outsourcing firm $14 per job today. You offer $10. They save money and sign.

Year one, people do most of the work, so it costs you $5.50. Fine — you still make money.

Every year the agents do more. Your cost falls. The price never changes.

Why this beats selling an AI product.

A software company grows profit by raising prices. That means a hard conversation every year.

You grow profit by getting better at your own work. The customer never notices.

4

The loop that shrinks your cost

This is the only clever bit, and it is simple. Four steps, over and over.

One rule you cannot break.

The checker in step 2 must be a person, or a simple hard-coded rule. Never another agent checking the first agent.

An agent checking its own work agrees with itself. Your dashboard says 96% correct while the customer gets angry.

5

Who pays you, and when

You are not asking anyone for new budget. You are taking money they already spend on an outsourcing firm.

Now → year 3

Companies pay you to get work done. Straight services money, like Etyme's customers paid for staffing. This is where all your money comes from at first.

Year 1 → 3

Companies pay for your software. They run their own agents and have no idea what they cost. You sell them the ledger you built for yourself.

Year 3 → 6

You take a cut of other people's agents. Other builders list on your platform. You test them and take a slice of what they earn.

Year 6 →

You become the rail. The "Stripe for agents" idea. It only works after the three steps above. Nobody can start here.

Sell services now. The platform gets built out of the receipts.

Sell to the VP of Operations. They own the outsourcing budget and are paid to make it smaller.

Do not sell to the CTO or Head of AI. They buy pilots. Pilots die.

Your easiest first customer is a staffing company — one of your old Etyme customers. Mountains of paperwork, scared of AI, and they already trust you.

6

What to do in the next 90 days

Do not rewrite Etyme. Do not build a platform. Get one customer paying for one type of job.

01

Pick one job type

One boring, repeated task a company already pays someone else to do. Onboarding paperwork. Invoice matching. Credential checks. Just one.

02

Write down what "done right" means

The hardest step, and it needs no code. If you can't write the checklist, you don't have a product yet. Do this before anything else.

03

Sell it before you build it

"You pay $14 and wait four days. We'll do it for $10, same day, guaranteed. Give us 10% of your volume." Do not mention AI.

04

Deliver with people first

Month one can be 90% people. You only have to beat the current vendor's price, and offshore people already can. Get paid while you learn.

05

Add the three new pieces

Job queue, agent, checker. Bolt them onto Etyme's existing invoicing and approvals. Then run the loop from section 4 every single week.

One honest warning about the old code.

Etyme runs on Ruby 2.6 and Rails 5.2. Both are past end of life and get no security fixes.

Fine for proving the business with one friendly customer. Not fine once real company data flows through it. Plan the upgrade for after your first paying customer — not before.

A hundred jobs

Is staffing dead? Where does demand come from? Where does supply come from? And 100 real jobs you could sell.

0

Is IT staffing dead?

No. I did not say that, and I don't believe it.

Placing senior engineers, cleared staff, on-site roles and regulated work is not going away. Companies will hire contract people for a long time yet.

Two narrower things are true:

One — commodity IT contract roles are shrinking. Junior dev, manual QA, basic support. That end gets squeezed.

Two — Etyme the software product is finished. Not because of AI. Because it's on a dead stack against Bullhorn, Ceipal and JobDiva who have 100 times your engineers. That fight was lost before AI arrived.

The staffing business isn't dead. Your software business is. Those are different problems.

So this isn't "replace staffing with robots." It's: you own a working back-office engine and 15 years of knowing how work turns into cash. Point it at a job that has budget today.

"There is zero demand"

You're right — and it doesn't matter. Nobody has a budget line called "AI agents." If that's what you sell, you get a pilot, then silence.

But the demand already exists under a different name. Every company you'd call is already paying someone to do this work.

So how do you get the first jobs?

Start with people who already know you. Your old Etyme customers are staffing firms. They drown in onboarding paperwork, timesheet chasing, compliance checks and invoice disputes. You know their pain exactly. No discovery calls needed.

Bid against a vendor, don't pitch a project. "You pay $14 and wait four days. I'll do it for $10, same day." That's a procurement conversation. Procurement people say yes to those.

Ask for 10% of the volume, not all of it. Run beside their current vendor. If you're worse, they lose nothing. This kills the risk objection dead.

Get paid per finished job. No setup fee, no licence, no project plan. They pay for output, so there's nothing to approve upfront.

Go mid-market, not enterprise. A big company takes 12–18 months to buy. A 200-person staffing firm decides in two weeks. You need cash before you need logos.

2

"There is zero supply"

Also true, and this one is easier than you think. You don't source agents. You build them.

In staffing, supply is the hard part. Good people are scarce, take weeks to find, cost a lot, and leave.

An agent is not scarce. You build it once for one job type. It runs forever and never resigns. It's a build problem, not a sourcing problem — and a much smaller one.

The trap to avoid.

Do not build a marketplace. A marketplace with no buyers and no sellers is the classic way to die.

Be one-sided. You are the supply. The customer is the demand. Other people's agents show up in year three, once there is money on the table worth showing up for.

Zero supply is fine when you are the supply.

How to pick your one

Don't pick the biggest market. Pick the one where you already know what "done right" means without asking anyone.

For you that's almost certainly the staffing rows — timesheet chasing, invoice matching, onboarding packets, insurance certificates, compliance checks. You've watched people do those for 15 years.

Your customers for those jobs are staffing firms. You already have their phone numbers.

Pick one row. Call three old customers. Ask what they pay for it today.

That's the whole next step. Not a build, not a deck. Three phone calls to find out the price you have to beat.

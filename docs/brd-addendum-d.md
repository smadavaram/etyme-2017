**D.1 THE OBSERVATION**

**The visa was never a benefit. It was the retention mechanism.**

Mid-tier consulting built its supply model on H1B and OPT candidates. Sponsorship performed three commercial functions simultaneously, none of which were ever priced or named:

> • It justified a below-market pay rate --- the visa itself was part of the compensation.
>
> • It made bench tolerable --- a consultant on bench retains status; a consultant who leaves may not.
>
> • It made departure expensive --- transfer friction, GC queue position, and timing risk all raised the cost of walking.

The consequence is structural, and it is the reason this addendum exists: because the visa held consultants in place, no attraction or retention capability was ever built. Two decades of supply-side operation produced deep sourcing muscle and effectively no employer-brand muscle.

**Removing the visa does not create a hiring problem. It exposes an absence.**

A US citizen or green card holder can leave on Friday. Nothing in the current model gives them a reason not to.

**D.2 WHY THE TRANSITION IS NOW FORCED**

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Pressure**                         **Effect on the H1B/OPT model**
  ------------------------------------ -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Cap odds and sponsorship cost        Per-hire acquisition cost rises while margin per placement falls. The arbitrage inverts.

  Client work-authorization mandates   Finance, healthcare, defense-adjacent and public-sector work increasingly requires citizen or GC status. Entire demand segments become unreachable.

  Rate compression                     The discount that visa candidates accepted is no longer sufficient to win on price alone.

  Recruiter attrition                  Sourcing headcount is the variable cost of the volume model. As hit rates fall, that headcount is cut first, and no new entrants replace it.
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Restated in the language of §1.2: the volume-arbitrage model is losing both of its inputs at once --- cheap supply and cheap sourcing labour. Firms respond by increasing submission volume, which is the one lever that has already stopped working.

**D.3 WHAT REPLACES THE VISA**

A retention mechanism for a labour pool that can leave must make the consultant genuinely better off. There is no coercive substitute. Seven mechanisms are specified across D.3.1 to D.3.7; all are product surface, not policy.

**D.3.1 Two kinds of margin**

**Transparency policy fails if it treats all margin as the same thing.**

The addendum distinguishes two margins that have been conflated throughout the baseline and throughout the industry:

+------------+---------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
|            | **Arbitrage margin**                        | **Expertise margin**                                   |
+============+=============================================+========================================================+
| Source     | Information asymmetry                       | Domain capability                                      |
|            |                                             |                                                        |
|            | Consultant lacks options or price knowledge | Fill speed, bench depth, client trust built over years |
+------------+---------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Mechanism  | Opacity is the margin                       | Capability is the margin                               |
|            |                                             |                                                        |
|            |                                             | Opacity is incidental                                  |
+------------+---------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Durability | Collapsing                                  | Defensible                                             |
|            |                                             |                                                        |
|            | Citizens and GC holders will not accept it  | Buyer pays for certainty, not for ignorance            |
+------------+---------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Function   | Extraction                                  | Funds reinvestment --- practice depth, training, bench |
+------------+---------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+

**The human capital thesis is only affordable on expertise margin.**

Coaching, reskilling and paid developing bench (D.3.5) cannot be funded from a commodity arbitrage spread. They can be funded from a domain practice margin. The vendor\'s stronghold domains are therefore not an exception to the platform thesis stated at §1.1 --- they are the only place it is economically viable. Margin funds the mentorship.

A platform-wide transparency mandate would destroy both margins to correct one. That is the reasoning behind D.3.2.

**D.3.2 Rate visibility --- a per-requirement setting**

Rate visibility is set at requirement level, inheriting a company default. It is not a company-wide switch and not a platform mandate.

> • Vendors disclose where disclosure wins supply --- hard-to-fill roles, citizen/GC target profiles, competitive metros.
>
> • Vendors withhold in stronghold domains, where they compete on fill speed and depth rather than on price legibility.
>
> • Setting lives on Requirement and is inherited by the resulting Assignment. Company default is configurable and overridable per requirement.

Rationale: an all-or-nothing company flag forces a choice most owners resolve as \'off\', and the mechanism dies unused. Per-requirement control makes disclosure a supply-acquisition tool the vendor deploys deliberately, rather than a concession extracted from them.

**D.3.3 Trust without disclosure**

**Selective disclosure can read as less trustworthy than none. Disclosure is therefore not the trust signal.**

Where a vendor does not disclose markup, trust is carried by mechanisms that cost the vendor nothing commercially:

> • Rate progression --- the consultant\'s own pay rate across assignments over time, visible to them without exposing bill rate or margin.
>
> • Bench pay honoured --- recorded instances of paid bench, a direct signal of how the vendor behaves when revenue stops.
>
> • Median tenure of consultants at the vendor, and time-to-next-placement after rolloff.
>
> • Verified record issuance (D.3.6) --- whether the vendor attests work history the consultant can take elsewhere.

Disclosure rate may be surfaced as an optional vendor badge. It is never required, and never displayed as an absence.

**D.3.4 Skill upgrade with real stakes**

Extends §7 (Training) from an acquisition funnel to a retention instrument. The candidate population changes from freshers entering the market to mid-career consultants with obsolescence risk.

> • TrainingProgram gains audience: acquisition \| retention \| transition.
>
> • Retention programs are offered to placed and benched consultants, not only to trainees.
>
> • Learning path targets AI-era role migration for consultants with tenure in a declining stack.

**D.3.5 Bench as investment, not obligation**

Under the H1B model, paid bench was a grudging DOL obligation (§10.4, bench_paid). Under the citizen/GC model it becomes an advertised benefit.

> • Assignment type bench_developing: paid bench with an attached TrainingEnrollment.
>
> • Bench burn dashboard (§6) distinguishes idle burn from developing burn --- the same cost, a different business meaning.
>
> • Vendor metric: proportion of bench days attached to a learning path.

**D.3.6 A portable, verified record**

Counterintuitive and load-bearing: retention improves when leaving is made easy, because portability signals confidence rather than capture.

> • Verified work history --- assignments, duration, client type, skills applied, attested by the vendor.
>
> • Consultant-owned and exportable, independent of continued engagement with the vendor.
>
> • This is the artifact that makes the consultant surface (D.4) worth returning to after a placement ends.

**D.3.7 A recruiter who knows them**

Direct implementation of the platform thesis stated at §1.1 --- recruiters as human capital developers rather than resume intermediaries. A consultant with options will not accept a rotating cast of sourcers.

> • Persistent recruiter-of-record on the consultant profile, surfaced to the consultant by name.
>
> • Recruiter performance metrics already include coaching completions (§20); D.7 extends these to retention outcomes.

**D.4 NEW SURFACE: THE CONSULTANT-SIDE APPLICATION**

**Gap identified: v3.7 contains no product built for the consultant.**

Every surface specified in the baseline serves the vendor, the client, or the platform operator. The consultant exists as a record --- a profile, a pipeline stage, a compliance object. This is sufficient for a captive supply pool and insufficient for a pool with alternatives.

+-------------------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| **Surface**       | **v3.7 baseline**                 | **Addendum D**                                           |
+===================+===================================+==========================================================+
| Candidate profile | Vendor-maintained record          | Consultant-maintained                                    |
|                   |                                   |                                                          |
|                   | Compliance and matching object    | Consultant-owned verified history                        |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Training          | Enrollment via vendor public site | Learning path with progression visible to the consultant |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Rate              | Internal to vendor                | Visible where vendor elects disclosure                   |
|                   |                                   |                                                          |
|                   |                                   | Progression band shown                                   |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Bench             | Vendor cost line                  | Development period with attached program                 |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Relationship      | Recruiter-owned record            | Named recruiter, visible to the consultant               |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+

Scope discipline: this is not a job board and not a second marketplace. It is a career record for consultants already engaged with a vendor on the platform. Public discovery remains the vendor site (§18).

**D.6 THE COMMERCIAL FILTER**

**Most firms will not adopt rate transparency, because opacity is the margin.**

This is stated plainly because it determines the addressable market rather than merely the feature set. Asking a mid-tier firm to disclose its markup is asking it to surrender the mechanism it has optimised around for two decades.

The consequence is a narrower and better-qualified customer definition:

+----------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **Segment**                                        | **Signal**                             | **Verdict**                                                       |
+====================================================+========================================+===================================================================+
| 100% H1B/OPT supply, no transition underway        | Has not decided to change              | Not a Phase 1--2 customer. Selling a decision they have not made. |
|                                                    |                                        |                                                                   |
|                                                    | Sees transparency as margin loss       |                                                                   |
+----------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Placing citizens/GC at any volume, however small   | Transition already begun               | Target. The pain is present and quantifiable.                     |
|                                                    |                                        |                                                                   |
|                                                    | Already experiencing the retention gap |                                                                   |
+----------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Direct client relationships, moving off tier-three | Margin no longer depends on opacity    | Strongest fit. Survives consolidation.                            |
+----------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------+

**Firms that cannot make this transition were unlikely to become customers under the baseline model either. The filter costs nothing that was reachable.**

**D.7 QUALIFICATION AND MEASUREMENT**

**D.7.1 Discovery questions**

Replaces generic pain discovery in the vendor sales motion (§27 GTM).

> • Submissions per placement last year, and this year. A doubling identifies the pain and confirms the buyer feels it.
>
> • Number of citizen or GC consultants currently placed. Zero indicates a transition not yet begun.
>
> • Attrition rate among non-sponsored consultants against sponsored. The delta is the retention gap, in the vendor\'s own numbers.

**D.7.2 Metrics added to the vendor dashboard**

> • Authorization mix --- sponsored against independent, trended.
>
> • Retention delta --- tenure of independent consultants against sponsored.
>
> • Development coverage --- proportion of bench days attached to a learning path.
>
> • Transparency adoption --- whether rate visibility is enabled, shown against retention outcome.

**D.9 OPEN QUESTIONS FOR RATIFICATION**

This addendum is proposed, not ratified. Four decisions remain with the founder:

> • Ratified: rate visibility is a per-requirement vendor setting, not a condition of membership. Remaining question --- should margin_class be vendor-declared, or inferred by the platform from fill time and domain concentration?
>
> • Does the consultant surface ship in Phase 2, or is a read-only verified record sufficient until Phase 3?
>
> • Does VerifiedRecord remain vendor-attested, or move toward third-party or client attestation? Vendor attestation is buildable now and worth less to the consultant.
>
> • Should the Phase 1 beachhead (§1.3) be restated in terms of authorization transition rather than firm size, given that transition status predicts fit better than headcount?

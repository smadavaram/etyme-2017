# AI agents and conversational assistants in contingent workforce software, 2025–2026

Research date: 2026-10-12. Scope: what VMS, MSP, ATS, HR-tech, payroll and AP vendors have shipped or announced as "agents" or copilots; what is already done without a UI (email, SMS, Slack/Teams); where vendors keep a human in the loop; what surveys say workers and recruiters want; and what law constrains an agent deciding alone. Facts only; vendor claims are labelled as such. No vendor here is a model to copy.

## Key question 1 — Which workforce, staffing, VMS, HR, payroll and AP vendors have announced agents or copilots in 2025–2026, and what do they do?

### Takeaway
Every major VMS (Beeline, Magnit, SAP Fieldglass, Workday VNDLY, Coupa) announced named agents between March 2025 and September 2026, but what shipped is mostly requisition drafting, candidate matching and Q&A; the agentic pieces that touch money (invoice validation, payroll variance) sit on the AP and payroll side (Coupa, ADP, Tipalti, Stampli, Ramp). Several of the VMS agents were still "early adopter" or "planned" at the time of announcement, and nearly every outcome figure is vendor-reported.

### Inferences
- The announced VMS agents cluster at the front of the process (requisition drafting, matching, knowledge Q&A). None of the VMS releases found describes an agent that approves hours, matches an invoice to signed hours, or watches a certificate expiry on its own; those functions appear as AP/payroll agents (Coupa invoice validation, ADP payroll variance) that flag and suggest, with a person approving.
- The 2026 moves by Beeline (MCP) and Coupa (Navi Connect, MCP) shift the design from "our agent in our UI" to "any agent may act through our permission model", which makes the VMS's role-based permissions and approval chain the real guardrail.
- Bullhorn's and LinkedIn's outcome figures are the only adoption numbers with a stated customer base, and all are vendor-sourced.

### Gaps
- Beeline MCP full release text (403): which VMS actions (create requisition, approve timesheet, query spend) are exposed to agents is not verified.
- No MSP (Allegis Global Solutions, Pontoon, Randstad Sourceright) announcement of agents in a program office was found; the search returned only provider profiles.
- Whether SAP's June 2026 Fieldglass Joule Agents and Workday's early-2026 Contingent Sourcing Agent GA actually shipped on schedule was not confirmed.
- Bill.com: no 2025–2026 agent announcement found.

## Key question 2 — What is already done "without a UI" (email approval, SMS check-ins, Slack/Teams, candidate chatbots), and what evidence of adoption exists?

### Takeaway
Email-link approval of contractor timesheets without a login, SMS scheduling and check-ins, and chat-window approvals in Slack/Teams all exist as shipped features; the measurable adoption evidence is strongest for SMS/chat scheduling (Paradox volumes, SIA's "a quarter interviewed by a bot") and weakest for chat-based approvals, where most integrations are notifications rather than recorded approvals.

### Cited Findings
- Email approval with no login, in an ATS: when a contractor submits a timesheet, PCRecruiter "automatically sends the configured Approver Form Letter to the designated approver. This email will contain a link for the approver to review the hours"; the approver enters initials, approves or rejects, and a rejection requires a note — [PCRecruiter docs](https://learning.pcrecruiter.net/site/docs/timesheets/approving-timesheets/)
- SAP Fieldglass: hiring managers "receive an email prompting them to access the Time Sheet in Fieldglass and take action" (login implied) — [Boston University manager guide](https://www.bu.edu/sourcing/files/2018/08/Manager-Guide-Time-and-Expense-Approval.pdf); the Manager Hub mobile app lets managers "approve time sheets, approve WO's and SOW's" — [SAP mobile apps](https://www.sap.com/india/products/spend-management/external-workforce-mobile-apps.html); a third party (Workgrid) sells approve/reject from a notification outside Fieldglass — [Workgrid](https://workgrid.com/template/sap-fieldglass/timesheet-approvals)
- Deel's plugin "brings core HR tasks into Slack and Microsoft Teams. Employees can request time off, track time… Managers review and approve directly", and "approvals made in Slack/Teams write back to Deel. Role-based rules, policies, calendars, and approval chains still apply" — [Deel plugin](https://www.deel.com/plugin/)
- Everhour: Slack is used for notifications/summaries; once a timesheet is approved "the time entries for that week are locked"; one vendor notes Slack summaries "do not replace complete and accurate wage and hour records" — [Everhour admin](https://support.everhour.com/article/473-time-approval-admin); [Everhour Slack](https://everhour.com/time-tracking/time-tracking-app-for-slack)
- Workday's Frontline Agent lets workers "report absences by text" and finds replacements — [Workday newsroom](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-05-19-Workday-Unveils-Next-Generation-of-Illuminate-Agents-to-Transform-HR-and-Finance-Operations)
- SMS check-ins: a staffing-SMS guide describes "periodic SMS check-ins to workers not currently on assignment" to keep availability current — [Notepage](https://www.notepage.net/learning-center/staffing-agency-sms.htm); Bond StaffSuite integrated Call-Em-All to auto-send SMS/voice to contingent employees — [RecruitingDaily](https://recruitingdaily.com/?p=20066)
- Paradox/Olivia schedules over SMS and chat; vendor volumes of 15.6M, 20M+ and 30M+ interviews scheduled appear across three Paradox/Workday documents — [Workday datasheet](https://www.workday.com/content/dam/web/en-us/documents/datasheets/paradox-for-workday-recruiting.pdf); [index.dev](https://index.dev/blog/paradox-ai-recruitment-chatbot-review)
- SIA North America Temporary Worker Survey 2025 (N=3,709): about 80% had never been interviewed by AI; the rest split across text-based (12%), voice (9%) and avatar (6%) recruiters; SIA summarises "roughly a fourth have been interviewed by a bot"; overall impression "moderately favorable" on a 1–6 scale; workers earning over $60/hour rated bots 5.0, those under $16/hour 2.9; satisfaction lower among temporary healthcare staff — [SIA webinar slides](https://www.staffingindustry.com/system/files/2025-08/whippy_americas_stl_webinar_final_082625_0.pdf); [SIA editorial](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/cws-30-contingent-workforce-strategies/what-do-contingent-workers-think-of-ai-bots-)
- TrueBlue/PeopleReady People Pulse Spring 2026 (1,200+ temporary workers): "just 3% of workers prefer AI-only communication about jobs", most favor human or a blend — [StreetInsider press release](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Temporary+Workers+Prioritize+Transparency+and+Human+Connection/26557711.html); [PeopleReady](https://www.peopleready.com/staffing-resources/people-pulse-spring-2026/)
- Fortune (August 2025) reported a study finding applicants interviewed by AI voice agents were 12% more likely to get an offer than those screened by humans (different study; measures outcome, not preference) — [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2025/08/22/ai-recruiters-could-be-the-unlikely-solution-to-career-catfishing-with-job-seekers-admitting-theyd-rather-interview-with-a-bot/)
- SIA's 2025 North American Staffing Executive Outlook: 73% of executives expected higher investment in automation and AI; 54% expected increased use of sourcing automation — [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/ai-growth-not-job-cuts-inside-sias-2025-staffing-outlook)

### Inferences
- The pattern that already works in the trade is: the system of record sends a one-time link or a chat card; the decision is written back into the record with name and time; chat is notification plus a button, not the record. Nobody found treats a free-text "ok" reply as the approval.
- Workers tolerate bots for scheduling and check-ins and dislike them for communication about the job itself; higher-paid workers (the IT contractor profile) rate bots better than low-wage workers.

### Gaps
- No source shows a supplier (as opposed to a client manager) approving a timesheet from an email link without login in a VMS.
- No independent adoption figures for Slack/Teams approvals; only vendor pages.
- No source on replying to an email ("approved") as a recorded approval in any named product.

## Key question 3 — Where do vendors keep a human in the loop (pay, compliance, rates), and what do they say about audit, co-employment, classification and privacy?

### Takeaway
Every vendor statement found puts a person on the final step for money and employment decisions — "suggest fixes that users can review and approve" (ADP), "humans stay in the loop for oversight and approval" (Coupa), "human oversight for high-stakes decisions" and "the same role-based permissions and approval processes" (Beeline) — while statements on co-employment and classification are thin: Beeline names "labor classification" as a risk area without saying how, and no vendor found describes audit logs of agent actions in public material.

### Cited Findings
- ADP: agents "designed to think, plan, and act with human oversight"; payroll fixes "subject to user review and approval"; built around "security, governance, and compliance expectations that enterprise HR and payroll workflows demand"; privacy not addressed — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/adp-launches-new-ai-agents-to-tackle-workforce-and-payroll-challenges)
- Beeline AI: "human-in-the-loop approach"; addresses "risks associated with labor classification, pay equity, and compliance"; co-employment and audit not mentioned — [Beeline](https://www.beeline.com/news/beeline-pioneers-a-new-generation-of-extended-workforce-management-with-beeline-ai); Beeline MCP: agents bound by "the same role-based permissions and approval processes that protect human users, with built-in identity verification and human oversight for high-stakes decisions" — [SHRM vendor directory](https://vendordirectory.shrm.org/company/923459/news/3867970/beeline-pioneers-agentic-ai-for-the-extended-workforce-with-beeline-mcp)
- Magnit Candidate Agent gives "guidance on candidate selections"; no approval steps, audit logs or bias testing described — [Magnit](https://aem.magnitglobal.com/us/en/resources/knowledge-center/blog/magnit-debuts-innovative-ai-agents-powered-by-maggi-to-optimize-business-outcomes.html)
- Coupa: "humans stay in the loop for oversight and approval"; Invoice Attachment Validation Agent "automates approve/reject decisions" on attachment-vs-record checks — [Coupa newsroom](https://www.coupa.com/newsroom/coupas-september-release-accelerates-agentic-spend-capabilities/)
- Deel: chat approvals keep "role-based rules, policies, calendars, and approval chains" — [Deel](https://www.deel.com/plugin/)
- Carly (staffing SMS agents): "optional approval rule before anything is sent" — [Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/ai-for-staffing-agencies/)
- A payroll vendor (Lano) describes the enterprise norm as "a strict Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) architecture. The AI handles the cognitive heavy lifting of data matching, cross-system communication, and pattern auditing" with a person holding final approval — [Lano](https://www.lano.io/blog/moving-past-the-chatbot-why-the-future-of-payroll-is-agentic); a Kredily commentary (August 2026) argues "Human-in-the-loop gates must be enforced outside the model for irreversible payroll actions" — [DailyAIWorld](https://dailyaiworld.com/blogs/kredilys-kai-rise-agentic-payroll-ai-runs-back-office)
- Workday: agents are managed through the "Agent System of Record", described as a central command center; the release does not state human oversight — [Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-05-19-Workday-Unveils-Next-Generation-of-Illuminate-Agents-to-Transform-HR-and-Finance-Operations)
- Liability precedent for a vendor's screening tool: in Mobley v. Workday the court (July 2024) let claims proceed on the theory that the vendor "may nonetheless be held liable as an 'agent'" of employers; on May 16, 2025 Judge Rita Lin granted preliminary certification of an ADEA collective of applicants aged 40+ "denied employment recommendations"; Workday's filings reportedly show ~1.1 billion applications rejected through its system in the period; merits unresolved — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/workday-ai-bias-lawsuit-class-collective-action/748518/); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/discrimination-lawsuit-over-workdays-ai-hiring-tools-can-proceed-as-class-action-6-things); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2025/05/ai-hiring-age-discrimination-federal-court-workday)

### Inferences
- The industry line is uniform: agents prepare, flag and suggest; a named person approves pay, a bill and a hire. The one agent found that "automates approve/reject decisions" (Coupa) does so on a document-consistency check, not on hours or rates.
- Mobley v. Workday is why vendors say "guidance" and "recommendation" about candidate ranking: a platform that scores candidates can be sued as the employer's agent even though it employs nobody.

### Gaps
- No vendor public material found that describes an audit log of agent actions (who/what/why) in a VMS; Beeline's "identity verification" is the nearest.
- No vendor statement found on co-employment risk specifically in connection with an agent acting on contractors.
- Privacy/data-residency statements for these agents were not found in the releases retrieved.

## Key question 4 — What do recruiters, program managers and contractors say they want from an assistant (SIA, SHRM, Gartner, Bersin, Deloitte, 2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
The free-tier evidence says staffing executives want automation of sourcing and admin (SIA 2025), HR leaders plan agentic AI broadly (Gartner, May 2025: 82% within 12 months), and workers want a human or a blend rather than AI-only contact (PeopleReady 2026: 3% prefer AI-only); the detailed "what tasks" data from SIA's internal-staff survey and Deloitte's 2026 report sits behind paywalls or was only found second-hand.

### Cited Findings
- SIA surveyed 16,311 internal staff at 258 North American staffing firms in 2025 on "Use of AI by Task, Perception of AI Helpfulness, and Requests to Management"; detailed findings are member-only — [SIA report page](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/north-america-internal-staff-survey-2025-use-of-ai-by-task-perception-of-ai-helpfulness-and-requests-to-management-); [SIA editorial](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/healthcare-staffing-report/sia-s-internal-staff-survey-reveals-rise-of-ai-and-employee-engagement-trends)
- SIA's "Global Staffing Middle and Back Office Software Landscape 2026 Update: AI Agents and the Digital Workforce" draws on responses from 39 middle/back-office products and "examines where agentic AI is moving into production" (member-only; fetch returned 403) — [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/global-staffing-middle-and-back-office-software-landscape-2026-update-ai-agents-and-the-digital-workforce)
- SIA 2025 executive outlook: 73% expect higher AI/automation investment; 54% expect more sourcing automation — [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/ai-growth-not-job-cuts-inside-sias-2025-staffing-outlook)

### Inferences
- The split is consistent: firms want agents for sourcing, screening and admin; workers accept bots for logistics (scheduling, check-ins) and want a person for anything about the job, pay or a decision.
- Nothing found measures what a program manager or an AP clerk wants from an assistant on timesheets, invoices or compliance specifically; that is a research gap the vendors' own feature choices (variance flags, invoice validation, expiry alerts) fill by implication only.

### Gaps
- SIA internal-staff "use of AI by task" percentages: paywalled.
- No 2025–2026 survey of hiring managers or program office staff about assistants for approvals, invoices or compliance was found.
- Gartner and SHRM figures were found only in secondary sources; the primary reports were not retrieved.

## Key question 5 — Reported failures and regulatory constraints on AI making employment decisions (EU AI Act, NYC LL144, Colorado) that limit what a "twin" may decide alone

### Takeaway
Three regimes converge on the same shape: an AI may screen, rank or recommend, but a person must be able to review and overturn, the worker must be told, and records must be kept — EU AI Act (high-risk employment obligations now delayed to December 2, 2027 by the Digital Omnibus), Colorado SB 26-189 (notice, human-review adverse-action process, three-year records, from January 1, 2027) and NYC LL144 (bias audit and notice for automated employment decision tools, weakly enforced per the State Comptroller's December 2025 audit). Mobley v. Workday shows the vendor itself can be sued over a screening recommendation.

### Cited Findings
- EU AI Act: high-risk HR uses include "recruitment and CV screening, candidate assessment and selection, performance management and monitoring, promotion and career progression decisions, disciplinary processes and dismissal"; employer duties include "meaningful human intervention in automated decision-making", transparency to employees and works councils, "recordkeeping, logging, and auditability", risk management and data governance for bias; prohibited practices and AI-literacy duties already apply; GDPR Article 22 runs in parallel; proposals delay high-risk obligations to late 2027/2028 — [Morgan Lewis, June 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/changes-to-eu-ai-act-deadlines-what-it-means-for-employers-and-hr-technology-providers)
- Digital Omnibus on AI: new deadlines December 2, 2027 for stand-alone high-risk systems (employment included) and August 2, 2028 for high-risk AI embedded in regulated products; Parliament approved June 16, 2026, Council formal approval pending at the time of writing, Official Journal publication expected before August 2026; "the delay is not a repeal" — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/eu-nears-approval-of-agreement-to-delay-rules-for-ai-use-in-employment-decisions/); [Remote Work Europe](https://remoteworkeurope.eu/news/2026/eu-digital-omnibus-ai-employment-rules-delay-december-2027). Final adoption after June 2026 was not confirmed in this research.
- EU Platform Work Directive: transposition due December 2, 2026; rebuttable presumption of employment for platform workers; first EU framework on algorithmic management with transparency duties and a right to human review of automated decisions — [Asanify digest, May 24, 2026](https://asanify.com/blog/news/colorado-ai-act-may-24-2026/)
- Colorado: SB 24-205 was first delayed to June 30, 2026 — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/colorado-postpones-implementation-of-colorado-ai-act-sb-24-205); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2025/colorado-delays-ai-act-to-june-2026-businesses-get-breathing-room/); then replaced by SB 26-189, signed May 14, 2026, effective January 1, 2027: for AI in "consequential" decisions such as hiring, employers must give notice before use, run an adverse-action process with a right to human review, and keep records for three years; impact assessments, risk-management programs, annual reviews and discrimination reports were removed; AG rulemaking due January 1, 2027 — [Asanify](https://asanify.com/blog/news/colorado-ai-act-may-24-2026/)
- NYC Local Law 144 (bias audits and notice for automated employment decision tools): the New York State Comptroller's audit (December 2, 2025) of DCWP enforcement for July 2023–June 2025 found 75% of test 311 calls about AEDTs were misrouted and never reached DCWP; DCWP reviewed 32 posted bias audits and flagged one issue where auditors found at least 17 potential problems; only two AEDT complaints were received in two years; DCWP agreed to most recommendations — [NY State Comptroller](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools); [beancount summary, June 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/04/nyc-local-law-144-aedt-bias-audit-compliance-state-hiring-ai-audit-patchwork-employers-2026-dcwp-enforcement-illinois-aivii-california-admt-maryland-hb-1202-guide). Penalties up to $1,500/day cited by a vendor guide; no 2026 penalty actions found.
- Mobley v. Workday: preliminary collective certification May 16, 2025 for applicants 40+ "denied employment recommendations" via Workday's platform since September 2020; vendor-as-agent theory allowed July 2024; disparate-impact claims survive, intentional-discrimination claims under California law dismissed; decertification possible after discovery — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/workday-ai-bias-lawsuit-class-collective-action/748518/); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/discrimination-lawsuit-over-workdays-ai-hiring-tools-can-proceed-as-class-action-6-things)
- An HR-tech blog on the EU AI Act: "if decision making is delegated to AI, there must be a human in the loop and a trace of decisions made by the tool… the responsibility will ultimately sit with the employer, not just the software badge" — [EasyStaff](https://easystaff.io/future-proofing-your-payroll-strategy-for-2026-and-beyond)

### Inferences
- For a product like Etyme, the constraint is on **who is chosen, ranked, rejected or let go**, not on **whether a bill matches signed hours**. An agent that reconciles an invoice to approved hours, chases a missing I-9 or counts contractors is outside every high-risk list found; an agent that ranks submissions or ends a placement is inside all three regimes and needs notice, a human who can overturn it, and a kept record.
- The regimes are converging on the same evidence Etyme already writes for other reasons: every automated act with a reason and a reversible flag (AutomationLog), every read logged (AccessLog), match scores with factors, basis, confidence and unknowns. The gap is worker-facing notice before an automated step and a human-review path on an adverse outcome.
- Enforcement is weaker than the statutes read (NYC's two complaints in two years; EU delayed; Colorado narrowed), so the practical pressure comes from litigation (Mobley) and enterprise procurement questionnaires rather than regulators.

### Gaps
- Whether the EU Digital Omnibus was formally adopted and published after June 2026 was not confirmed.
- Illinois HB 3773 (AI in employment, effective January 1, 2026) and California's ADMT regulations appear in a secondary summary but were not researched here.
- No reported failure of an agent on timesheets, invoices or compliance (as opposed to hiring) was found in trade press.

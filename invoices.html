Invoices & the three-way match

A bill is paid when three things agree: the hours a manager signed, the order line those hours were bought against, and the invoice itself. Where they do not agree, nothing is paid and the reason is a sentence.

Key capabilities

Invoice from signed hours

Supplier invoices only from weeks the client already signed.

Three-way match

Signed week, order line, invoice. All must match. No match, no pay.

Over the ceiling

Bill past the order cap is refused. Nobody overrides it.

No signed week

Held, not refused. Finance sees which team owns it.

Due dates

From day the client received it.

Money flows two ways

Supplier invoiced against order. Employee paid by payroll. Nobody sees a higher rate.

The complaint

“Three suppliers bill us on three cycles into two inboxes. By the time finance has the quarter assembled, the quarter is over, and nobody can say which invoice belongs to which signed week.”

The finance desk at a company buying through more than one staffing supplier

Every supplier’s invoice is right by its own lights. The problem is that each was built from its own copy of the hours, keyed in again at each rung of the chain, and the copy finance sees is the last one rather than the one the client actually approved.

What Etyme does

The signed week is the receipt. Two companies sign it, and one row of hours travels the whole chain. Nobody re-keys it, so the hours the client approved are the hours everybody bills from.

The firm bills its customer from hours that are already signed. The due date belongs to the document and is counted from the day the client received it. A bill with no signed week behind it, or no room left on the order line, is refused in words rather than parked in a queue.

Money leaves in two directions. A supplier invoice is received and matched against the order behind it. An employee is paid by payroll instead. The person sees what they are paid, and never what a firm above them charges.

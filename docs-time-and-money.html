Time and money

From an hour worked to a bill paid. Seven stations, one record, every step visible.

The complete flow

This is how Etyme follows every contractor through the entire payment cycle. Each of the seven stations below is one step from work to payment, with rules that cannot be overridden and visibility that keeps everybody honest.

Desktop workflow

Understanding the flow

Step 1: Work begins

A contractor starts work on an assignment. Hours are logged, time is tracked. Nothing is delayed waiting for approval yet.

Step 2: Timesheet filed

The contractor files their weekly timesheet showing hours worked. The form captures all the details needed downstream.

Step 3: Manager approval

The hiring manager signs the week, confirming the hours match what was expected. Both the candidate and manager must approve—never one alone.

Step 4: System flags

The system checks against rules: Is the rate within bounds? Are hours within reasonable daily limits? Are there contract violations? All flagged immediately.

Step 5: Invoice issued

The supplier invoices based on the approved, signed week. Not from copies or estimates—from the actual hours the client already approved.

Step 6: Three-way match

Finance checks: Does the signed week exist? Does the invoice amount match the order? Is there room left on the order line? All three, or nothing is paid.

Step 7: Payment

Money flows two ways: The supplier is paid by the client for the invoice. The contractor is paid by payroll—never seeing the higher rate a firm above them charges.

What cannot be overridden

Every rule in this flow has a reason. None can be bypassed by an approver or a manager:

  • No self-approval. A contractor never approves their own timesheet. A manager never approves their own expenses.
  • No unsigned bills. An invoice without a signed, approved week behind it is refused, never parked for later.
  • No overages. A bill past the order cap is rejected. The order must be raised or the supplier re-issues. Nobody overrides the line.
  • No hidden rates. An employee is paid through payroll. They never see the markup a staffing supplier charges above their rate.
  • Every flag is visible. Violations are not hidden in a queue or sent to an inbox. They appear on every view.
